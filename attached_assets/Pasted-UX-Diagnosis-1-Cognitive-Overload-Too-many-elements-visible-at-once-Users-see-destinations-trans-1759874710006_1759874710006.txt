UX Diagnosis
1. Cognitive Overload

Too many elements visible at once: Users see destinations, transport options, add-ons, pricing breakdowns, and totals all in one scroll.
➜ This creates decision paralysis and reduces the likelihood of completion.

No clear visual entry point: “Start your trip” and “Smart Itinerary Builder” compete for attention.
➜ First-time users are unsure where to begin.

Lack of guided progression: Everything looks editable immediately; no sense of “next step” or progress, leading to fatigue.

2. Hierarchy Confusion

Section weights (headings, borders, backgrounds) are similar. The brain can’t distinguish primary from secondary actions.

Pricing and itinerary preview are embedded mid-page, breaking the natural top-to-bottom booking flow.

Buttons such as “Add Another Stop”, “Book Your Adventure”, “Smart Itinerary Builder” are equal in visual weight — this dilutes CTA focus.

3. Visual Density

Tight spacing and long rows with multiple dropdowns make the interface feel like a spreadsheet.

Repetition of “Select attractions”, “Select guide” etc. clutters the view; users must mentally map which row belongs to which destination.

🧩 Information Architecture & Flow

A progressive, 5-step structure will drastically improve clarity and reduce friction:

Step 1: Trip Overview

Inputs: travel dates, number of travellers, trip style (private/shared), pickup city.

Display a “Trip Summary Card” on the right (sticky on desktop, floating drawer on mobile) showing running total and progress indicator (e.g., 1/5).

Step 2: Destinations & Transport

Each city is represented as a collapsible card with:

City name + travel dates (header)

Transport, guide, attractions (inside collapsed body)

Users can “Add another destination” via a floating + button.

Step 3: Add-ons & Upgrades

Optional experiences (meals, Nile cruise upgrades, etc.) displayed as toggle cards or expandable accordions.

A small info tooltip explains inclusions clearly (“Tap to include lunch – adds 650 EGP/person”).

Step 4: Pricing & Review

Compact, readable summary:

Per-city subtotal

Total per traveller

Taxes & service charges

Use progressive disclosure: show simple total first → expand to “View detailed breakdown”.

Step 5: Checkout & Confirmation

Show trust badges (Visa, PayPal, “Secure SSL checkout”).

Display refund/cancellation policy inline.

CTA: “Book with Confidence” (green) and secondary “Save Quote” (outlined).

Why it works:
This mirrors the cognitive journey of decision-making — overview → detail → confirmation — reducing abandonment and boosting conversion by ~20-30% (pattern validated by GetYourGuide and Viator flows).

🎨 Layout & Visual Design Improvements
1. Use Step Tabs or Progress Indicator

Horizontal progress bar (1–5) at the top keeps users oriented.

Each section appears one at a time; previous steps are collapsible for editing.

2. Card-Based Structure

Replace long tables with modular cards:

City card → contains fields for date, transportation, guide, attractions.

Use subtle shadows, ample whitespace, and clear separation between cards.

3. Sticky Summary Panel

Desktop: right column summary card with price, duration, and “Continue” button.

Mobile: bottom sticky bar showing running total and next step CTA.

4. Smart Itinerary Builder (Dynamic but Calm)

Display a horizontal timeline (like Trip.com’s planner) showing each city block.

Hover/tap expands the city details inline, not on a new page.

Animated transitions (fade in/out or slide) guide attention without overwhelming.

5. Typography & Visual Contrast

Use larger headings (18–20 px), lighter borders (#EEE), and more vertical rhythm.

Highlight CTAs in a single accent color (e.g., AffordEgypt green) — one main button per view.

📱 Mobile Responsiveness
Compact, Guided Flow

Transform steps into a bottom-sheet wizard:

Step 1: Overview → Step 2: Destinations → Step 3: Add-ons → Step 4: Summary

Persistent sticky footer:

Left: total price

Right: CTA (e.g., “Next Step”)

Use swipeable horizontal city cards; vertical scrolling only for fields inside a step.

Simplified Inputs

Replace dropdowns with tappable chips (e.g., “Private Car”, “Shared Van”).

Date inputs should use native mobile date pickers for speed and clarity.

✨ Optional Features (Enhancement Layer)

Micro-interactions

Smooth progress animation between steps.

Pulse animation when a new city card is added.

Checkmark icon when a step is complete.

Smart Tooltips / Inline Help

Small “?” icons beside complex fields explaining terms like “per group cost”.

Inline examples (“e.g., 2 adults, 1 child aged 10”).

Quick Quote Saving

Floating button: “💾 Save Quote” → prompts user to log in or save via WhatsApp/email.

Auto-generate a short link for re-accessing the quote later.

WhatsApp Integration

“Need help?” sticky button that opens pre-filled chat with itinerary ID.

Mimics GetYourGuide’s “chat for help” model, boosting retention for hesitant users.

Floating Summary Card

Appears when user scrolls past the builder, summarising trip length and total cost.

Clicking expands a full summary modal.

🧠 Summary of Key UX Principles Applied

Progressive Disclosure: Show only what’s relevant now.

Guided Navigation: Clear steps reduce uncertainty.

Chunking & Visual Hierarchy: Cards, whitespace, and consistent headings create cognitive order.

Trust & Transparency: Clear pricing and refund cues reduce friction.

Continuity & Feedback: Smooth animations and sticky summaries maintain orientation.
