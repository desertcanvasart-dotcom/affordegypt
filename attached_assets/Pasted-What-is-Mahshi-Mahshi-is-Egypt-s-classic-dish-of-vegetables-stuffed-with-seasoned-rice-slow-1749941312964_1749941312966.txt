What is Mahshi?
Mahshi (محشي) is Egypt’s classic dish of vegetables stuffed with seasoned rice, slow-cooked in a light tomato broth. The name literally means “stuffed”, and it typically includes vegetables like zucchini, eggplant, bell peppers, tomatoes, cabbage leaves, and grape leaves.

Mahshi is a dish deeply tied to family gatherings, feasts, and celebrations, often prepared in large trays to share. It’s vegetarian by default, though variations with minced meat exist.

🌟 Key Features:
Stuffed vegetables: zucchini, eggplant, bell peppers, grape or cabbage leaves

Filling: rice mixed with tomato, herbs, and spices

Slow-cooked: in a tomato-based broth for maximum flavour

Flavour: herby, garlicky, rich, and comforting

Naturally vegetarian, often vegan

📝 Traditional Egyptian Mahshi Recipe (Serves 5–6)
🧂 Ingredients:
For the Vegetables:
6 small zucchini

6 small bell peppers (or mix of eggplants, tomatoes, grape leaves, etc.)

1 large potato (optional, sliced as a base layer)

For the Rice Stuffing:
1½ cups Egyptian rice (short grain), rinsed

1 large onion, finely grated

2–3 tbsp tomato paste

1 bunch parsley, finely chopped

1 bunch dill, finely chopped

1 bunch cilantro, finely chopped

2 tbsp vegetable oil or ghee

1 tsp salt

½ tsp black pepper

½ tsp ground coriander

½ tsp cumin

Optional: chili flakes

For the Cooking Sauce:
2 cups water or vegetable broth

2 tbsp tomato paste

Salt and pepper to taste

🍳 Instructions:
1. Prepare the Vegetables
Zucchini & eggplant: hollow out carefully using a vegetable corer.

Bell peppers: remove tops and seeds.

Tomatoes: hollow and reserve the juice for the broth.

If using cabbage or grape leaves, blanch them in hot water until soft and pliable.

2. Make the Stuffing
In a bowl, mix the rice, tomato paste, onion, herbs, oil/ghee, and all spices.

Do not cook the rice — it will cook inside the vegetables as they simmer.

3. Stuff the Vegetables
Fill each vegetable about 2/3 full to allow the rice to expand.

Arrange them upright in a deep pot, layering with optional potato slices at the bottom to prevent sticking.

4. Prepare the Broth
Mix water or broth with tomato paste, salt, and pepper.

Pour it into the pot until it reaches about ¾ up the sides of the vegetables.

5. Cook the Mahshi
Cover the pot tightly and bring to a gentle simmer.

Cook on low heat for 45–60 minutes, or until vegetables are tender and rice is fully cooked.

6. Serve Warm
Carefully transfer the Mahshi to a serving tray.

Spoon some broth on top and enjoy with yogurt or green salad.

🧺 Tips & Variations:
You can add minced meat to the rice for a richer version.

Mahshi is often served at Friday family lunches, Eid, and special occasions.

Cabbage and grape leaves versions are especially loved in Upper Egypt and the Delta.

Serve with lemon wedges, pickles, or molokhia on the side for a complete meal.
