What is Molokhia?
Molokhia (ملوخية), also spelled Mulukhiyah or Molokheya, is a deep green leafy soup or stew made from the finely chopped leaves of the jute mallow plant. It’s one of the most ancient and beloved dishes in Egypt, with a history that dates back to Pharaonic times.

Smooth, garlicky, and full of umami, Molokhia is commonly served with rice and meat (usually chicken or rabbit) or sometimes just with baladi bread and lemon. Its unique, slightly mucilaginous (slippery) texture is an acquired taste, but for Egyptians, it’s the ultimate comfort food.

🌟 Key Characteristics:
Texture: Thick and slightly viscous (similar to okra)

Flavour: Earthy, garlicky, and rich with coriander

Commonly served with: White rice, vermicelli rice, or flatbread

Protein pairings: Chicken, rabbit, beef, or seafood

Best enjoyed with: A squeeze of lemon and hot chili vinegar

📝 Egyptian Molokhia Recipe (Serves 4–5)
🧂 Ingredients:
400–500g molokhia leaves, finely chopped (fresh or frozen)

6–8 cloves garlic, finely minced

1 tbsp ground coriander

3 cups chicken broth (or beef/vegetable broth)

2 tbsp ghee or butter

Salt, to taste

Juice of ½ lemon

Optional: 1 tsp dried coriander leaves or chili flakes

📝 If using fresh molokhia: Wash, dry thoroughly, and chop until paste-like with a sharp knife or mezzaluna.

🍳 Instructions:
1. Prepare the Broth Base
Bring the broth to a gentle simmer in a pot.

Season lightly with salt and a pinch of black pepper.

2. Add the Molokhia
Add the chopped molokhia (or thawed frozen molokhia) to the hot broth.

Stir only once and then turn the heat to very low — do not boil.

Heat for about 3–5 minutes until it’s warmed through and thickened.

❗ Avoid boiling Molokhia or it can lose its signature texture and turn slimy.

3. Make the Tasha (Garlic-Coriander Mix)
In a small pan, melt the ghee or butter.

Add minced garlic and ground coriander.

Sauté over medium heat until garlic is golden and aromatic (about 2–3 minutes).

4. Add Tasha to the Molokhia
Carefully pour the hot tasha into the molokhia pot.

It will sizzle — this is key to infusing the flavour.

Stir once, adjust seasoning, and remove from heat.

5. Serve Immediately
Pour over a plate of rice or serve with Egyptian baladi bread.

Squeeze lemon juice on top for brightness.

Serve with grilled chicken, rabbit, or even hard-boiled eggs.

🍽️ Variations:
Alexandrian-style: Includes chopped tomatoes and is often served with seafood.

Palestinian/Lebanese style: Whole leaves are used, texture is more stew-like.

With rabbit: A traditional rural Egyptian pairing, often used for feasts.

Some households serve it with a small bowl of vinegar-chili dip on the side.
